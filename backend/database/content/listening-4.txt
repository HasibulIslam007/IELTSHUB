Demonstration audio. Part 4. Today we will examine the restoration of Brook Lane Stream. For decades it flowed through a concrete channel built to reduce flooding near factories. In 2015, the council began removing sections of concrete. Engineers widened the channel and created shallow banks. These banks were planted with native grasses. Instead of carrying water away as quickly as possible, the new design lets it spread into a meadow during heavy rain. The meadow normally serves as public open space. Biologist Sarah Dean monitored the project for five years. She measured water temperature every hour and counted aquatic insects each spring. Shade from new trees reduced the highest summer temperature by two degrees. The team found nine species of aquatic insects in the first year and seventeen in the fifth. However, water quality still declined after storms because polluted runoff entered from roads. Dean recommended filtering that runoff before it reaches the stream. The project cost two million pounds. Most funding came from a regional environment fund, with a smaller contribution from the council. Local volunteers now remove litter once a month.
